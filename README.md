# Anti-Corrosion Coatings on Galvanised Steel Investigation

**File:** `corrosion-coatings.pdf`
**Subject:** Chemistry

## Research Question
How effective are different coatings (chain lubricant, vegetable oil, coconut oil, WD-40, and no coating) at preventing corrosion of a galvanised steel screw when exposed to 0.1 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid, as measured by the percentage difference in the screw's width, length, and mass using a digital calliper and an electronic balance?

## Overview
This investigation simulates acid-rain corrosion of the galvanised steel screws commonly used on go-karts, particularly relevant to karts raced near ports where sulfur oxide emissions contribute to acid rain.

- Coated 25 galvanised steel screws (5 per group) in one of five treatments, then submerged all screws in 0.1 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid for 14 days to accelerate corrosion.
- Measured length, width, and mass before and after exposure using a digital calliper (±0.01 mm) and electronic balance (±0.01 g).
- Converted raw changes into percentage differences (normalising for each screw's initial dimensions) and applied full uncertainty propagation.
- Used the interquartile range (IQR) method to flag outliers in the dataset.

## Key Findings
- All four coatings reduced corrosion relative to the uncoated control across all three measured dimensions.
- **Coconut oil** performed best among the natural/household coatings, with the lowest average length corrosion (0.60%) and mass loss (11.03%), attributed to its higher viscosity forming a thicker, more uniform non-polar barrier.
- **WD-40** gave the best overall (equally-weighted length/width/mass) protection, though it showed no improvement over the control in length corrosion specifically, possibly due to uneven application.
- **Chain lubricant** had the lowest width corrosion but higher mass loss, likely due to evaporation of its shorter-chain hydrocarbons.

## Limitations
- Screws were pat-dried rather than air-dried, risking uneven coating removal (systematic error).
- Sulfuric acid concentration and evaporation in the fume cupboard were not independently verified.
- Comparative design (rather than testing a range of concentrations/temperatures) limits how generalisable the ranking of coatings is.
